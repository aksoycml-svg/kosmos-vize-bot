## 0. Spine

**Turn:** Before: the drummer is carried to the morgue as dead; fifteen players are left / After: the same drummer is on stage, and the whole city is listening. Type: **reversal.**

**Hero:** conductor Karl Eliasberg. Aydarov is the man he finds: the hook's question and the ending's answer.

1. **Situation:** Leningrad under siege; about fifteen of the radio orchestra's musicians are left.
2. **Want:** to perform the symphony he has been asked to play. It needs nearly a hundred.
3. **Obstacle:** hunger. A drummer, Aydarov, collapses and is taken to the morgue as dead.
4. **Change:** Eliasberg goes down to say goodbye and finds him alive.
5. **Result:** on concert day the guns protect the hall, the lights come on, loudspeakers carry the music, and Aydarov is among the drummers.

**Last line (written first):** "They played to the end. Among the drummers was Aydarov, back from the morgue."

## 1. Accuracy notes (EN-specific)
- "the story goes" in narration (P06); source label on screen (P08). No "list of the dead", no "corpse cart".
- "drummer / drummers", never "snare drum". "Among the drummers": four percussionists in the line-up.
- "about fifteen", "nearly a hundred", "hundreds of thousands". No shell count, no operation code name, no Astoria banquet, no German-soldier quote.
- "toward the German lines" (broadcast was directed there; we do not claim it was heard).
- Year appears on screen and may be spoken in EN if wanted; this draft keeps it on screen only, to match the TR picture.

## 8. Publishing
- **Cover:** THE DRUMMER FROM THE MORGUE (5 words)
- **Caption:** On 9 August 1942, in besieged Leningrad, conductor Karl Eliasberg led Shostakovich's Seventh Symphony. Of the radio orchestra's 40 players, about 15 were left in the city; the rest were recalled from the front and from military bands. The concert was broadcast by loudspeaker across the city and toward the German lines. The story of percussionist Zhavdet (Jaudat) Aydarov comes from the research of historian Yulia Kantor and from his grandson Nadim Aydarov; details differ between tellings (in some the conductor sees a finger move, in others he hears him breathe). Aydarov is reported to have lived until 2000. In 1964 the surviving players met again in the same hall, with instruments placed on the chairs of those who had died. Scenes and gestures are dramatised; no recording of the symphony is used. Narration is AI-generated. Sources: Wikipedia (Leningrad première, Karl Eliasberg, Siege of Leningrad), St. Petersburg Philharmonia, Y. Kantor.
