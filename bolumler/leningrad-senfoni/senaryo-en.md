## 0. Spine

**Turn:** Before: the drummer lies among the dead and the conductor comes to say goodbye / After: the same drummer is on stage, playing the symphony to the end. Type: **reversal.**

**Hero:** conductor Karl Eliasberg, the single hero. Aydarov is his drummer; the narration names the link in P06, P07, P09 and P15.

1. **Situation:** the German army has besieged Leningrad; Eliasberg has about fifteen musicians left.
2. **Want:** to perform Shostakovich's new symphony. It needs nearly a hundred.
3. **Obstacle:** hunger. The first rehearsal lasts fifteen minutes, three musicians die, the drummer Aydarov collapses and Eliasberg hears he is dead.
4. **Change:** in the morgue Eliasberg sees Aydarov's finger move and pulls him out. Aydarov rejoins; the score arrives by plane.
5. **Result:** on concert day the guns protect the hall, the lights come on, loudspeakers carry the concert, the audience stands for the exhausted players, and Aydarov is on the drums.

**Last line (written first):** "The orchestra played to the end. On the drums was Aydarov, the man Eliasberg went to say goodbye to."
It answers the hook from the other side instead of repeating it; the hook's literal payoff ("pulled him out") comes mid-film, in P09.

## 8. Publishing
- **Cover:** PULLED FROM AMONG THE DEAD (5 words)
- **Caption:** On 9 August 1942, in Leningrad under German siege, conductor Karl Eliasberg led Shostakovich's Seventh Symphony. Of the radio orchestra's 40 players, about 15 were left in the city; the rest were sent from the front and from military bands. The first rehearsal was cut short after fifteen minutes, and three players died during the rehearsal period. The score was flown in that July. The concert was broadcast by loudspeaker across the city and toward the German lines. Zhavdet (Jaudat) Aydarov was one of the orchestra's four percussionists. His story comes from the research of historian Yulia Kantor and from his grandson Nadim Aydarov; details differ between tellings (in most the conductor sees a finger move, in others he hears him breathe). Aydarov is reported to have lived until 2000. In 1964 the surviving players met again in the same hall, with instruments placed on the chairs of those who had died. Scenes and gestures are dramatised; no recording of the symphony is used. Narration is AI-generated.
