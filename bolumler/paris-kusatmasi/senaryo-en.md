## 9. Sharing
- **Cover (max 5 words):** BALLOON OUT, PIGEON IN
- **Caption:** In September 1870, Prussian armies surrounded Paris and the telegraph went dead; roughly two million people were trapped inside. Balloons carried mail out but drifted with the wind and could not return. Replies came in by pigeon: pigeons were carried out by balloon and, released far away, flew home to their lofts. Since a bird can barely carry any weight, photographer René Dagron's method put pages onto finger-sized film, tied to the tail and enlarged on a screen in Paris to be copied by hand. Figures vary by source: messages that arrived are given as roughly 60,000 to 150,000; the balloon count is about 66; Paris's population and the Ville d'Orléans distance are approximate. The Ville d'Orléans balloon, bound for Tours, landed in the snowy mountains of Norway; the pilot and courier survived. What happened to its mail afterwards is not clear in the sources. That its basket is now in an Oslo museum (Norsk Teknisk Museum) rests on a search summary. Scenes and gestures are dramatized. Narration is AI-generated. Sources: Smithsonian Air & Space, French Ministry of Defence "Chemins de mémoire," Norsk Teknisk Museum, UCLA "Pigeon Post into Paris" exhibit.
