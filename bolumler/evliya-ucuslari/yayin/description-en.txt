Two flights, one witness: Lagâri and Hezarfen

In the 1630s, under Sultan Murad IV, at the celebration for the birth of the sultan's daughter, Lagâri Hasan Çelebi is said to have flown off Istanbul's Sarayburnu on a gunpowder rocket and landed in the sea in front of the sultan's pavilion. Hezarfen Ahmed Çelebi is said to have glided from the Galata Tower across the Bosphorus to Üsküdar, and was then exiled to Algiers. Both flights are known from one source only: the Ottoman traveler Evliya Çelebi, in his Seyahatname (Book of Travels). Whether Evliya saw them himself is not known, and no other record has been found. Dates vary by source; the height (~250 m) and distance (~3 km) are modern estimates. The TDV Islamic Encyclopedia explains the nickname "Hezarfen" as "of a thousand sciences"; "a thousand skills" is a plain rendering. (Çelebi is an honorific, "gentleman"; Jesus, Isa, is a prophet in Islam too.)
