# Script EN v1: Evliya Flights — "Two men flew over Istanbul, they say. Only one wrote it down."

Source: approved `senaryo.md` (TR) + `arastirma.md`. Channel: EN. Same 14 plans, same numbers, same visuals, same decisions as TR. Not a literal translation: English rhythm, short spoken sentences.
Format: 1080x1920 · plan timing identical to TR (59.5 s) · **132 words** (≈52.8 s speech at 2.5 w/s; TR 106 words ≈50.5 s at 2.1 w/s) · locations: SARAYBURNU (seaside pavilion + shore, night), GALATA-TOP (tower top, day), BOSPHORUS (wide + map line), BOOK-INSERT (Seyahatname).

Accuracy locks (same as TR):
- Evliya never "saw" or "witnessed". He is the one who wrote it down. The hook says "flew" + "they say" (English stand-in for the Turkish -mış hearsay ending), so it never reads as a flat claim.
- Lagâri does not cross the Bosphorus: he lifts off at Sarayburnu and lands in the sea in front of the pavilion. Only Hezarfen crosses. "Exile" is used only for Hezarfen.
- Not used: "brother", "measured the wind", "first rocket man", distance measuring, "skinny" (single source).
- Wings are never described (neither Lagâri's nor Hezarfen's): plain, vague silhouette. The word "wings" only. "Seven-armed" is not said; the rocket is a neutral bundle of gunpowder tubes.
- Two quoted lines (Lagâri, sultan) are Evliya's reported words, simplified. They are marked as dramatization in the description and on the name tags.
- P06 bundle-drop (EV-D2), P05 helper's hat (EV-D1) and other touches are dramatization (description says so).

## 0. Story spine (same as TR)
1. **Curiosity:** two men flew over Istanbul. Only one person wrote it down. Who, and is it true?
2. **Flight 1, Lagâri:** the sultan's daughter is born, fireworks. The sultan watches from his seaside pavilion. Lagâri sits on a gunpowder rocket.
3. **How:** the powder pushes him up. When it runs out he opens his wings, glides, lands in the sea in front of the pavilion. Reward: coins and a salary.
4. **Flight 2, Hezarfen:** from the top of Galata Tower, on the lodos wind, he glides across the Bosphorus to Üsküdar.
5. **Link:** same sultan, same pavilion.
6. **Twist:** Hezarfen gets gold, then exile. Reason: "this man is to be feared."
7. **Answer:** the one writer is Evliya Çelebi. Nobody else.
8. **Close and loop:** "No proof. Yet we've told it for 400 years." Screen asks "Did they really fly?" The spark on the book drawing cuts back to P01.

**Why K1:** it sets the angle (one witness) in the first two seconds and opens two questions: "who wrote it?" (answered P13) and "did they really fly?" (left to the viewer, P14). "They say" carries the hearsay the Turkish -mış carries, so it is not a flat claim, and costs only two words. "Only one wrote it down" stays punchy and true. The last line "we've told it for 400 years" flows straight back into "Two men flew over Istanbul, they say" on re-watch, so the loop works in speech too. K2 has the stronger image, but it only tells Lagâri's half and does not loop; its image is already K1's first frame. K3 gives away the "same pavilion" twist that P10 needs.
**Fallback K1b (no hedge, 10 words, 4.0 s):** "Two men flew over Istanbul. Only one wrote it down." See user question 1.

## 8. Sharing
- **Cover (≤5 words):** TWO FLIGHTS, ONE WITNESS
- **Description:** In the 1630s, under Sultan Murad IV, Lagâri Hasan Çelebi is said to have flown off Istanbul's Sarayburnu on a gunpowder rocket and landed in the sea in front of the sultan's pavilion. Hezarfen Ahmed Çelebi is said to have glided from the Galata Tower across the Bosphorus to Üsküdar. Both flights are known from one source only: the Ottoman traveler Evliya Çelebi, in his Seyahatname (Book of Travels). Whether Evliya saw them himself is not known, and no other record has been found. (Çelebi is an honorific, "gentleman"; Jesus, Isa, is a prophet in Islam too.) Dates vary by source; the height (~250 m) and distance (~3 km) are modern estimates. The two quotes are Evliya's reported words, simplified. All scenes and gestures, including the helper's hat and the dropped bundle, are dramatization. The voice is AI-generated. Sources: TDV Islamic Encyclopedia (M. Kaçar, "Hasan Çelebi, Lâgarî" and "Hezarfen Ahmed Çelebi"), Küre Encyclopedia.
