# Script EN ONAYLI v2 (2026-11-07): Evliya Flights — "A man rode a rocket into the sky over Istanbul. Another glided across the Bosphorus."

Source: approved `senaryo.md` (TR) + `arastirma.md`. Channel: EN. Same 14 plans, same numbers, same visuals, same decisions as TR. Not a literal translation: English rhythm, short spoken sentences.
Format: 1080x1920 · plan timing identical to TR (61.0 s) · **138 spoken words** ("400" counted as "four hundred"; ≈55.2 s speech at 2.5 w/s; TR 115 words ≈54.8 s at 2.1 w/s) · locations: SARAYBURNU (seaside pavilion + shore, night), GALATA-TOP (tower top, day), BOSPHORUS (wide + map line), BOOK-INSERT (Seyahatname).

Accuracy locks (same as TR):
- Evliya never "saw" or "witnessed". He is the one who wrote it down. The hook ("rode a rocket… glided across") is flat, unhedged (user decision); the hedge is carried by the screen text ("ONE WITNESS: A BOOK"), P13 ("Only from Evliya Çelebi's book") and the "So, no proof." ending instead of the Turkish -mış ending.
- Lagâri does not cross the Bosphorus: he lifts off at Sarayburnu and lands in the sea in front of the pavilion. Only Hezarfen crosses. "Exile" is used only for Hezarfen.
- Not used: "brother", "measured the wind", "first rocket man", distance measuring, "skinny" (single source).
- Wings are never described (neither Lagâri's nor Hezarfen's): plain, vague silhouette. The word "wings" only. "Seven-armed" is not said; the rocket is a neutral bundle of gunpowder tubes.
- Two quoted lines (Lagâri, sultan) are Evliya's reported words, simplified. They are marked as dramatization in the description and on the name tags.
- P06 bundle-drop (EV-D2), P05 helper's hat (EV-D1) and other touches are dramatization (description says so).

## 0. Story spine (same as TR)
1. **Curiosity:** one man rode a rocket into the sky, another glided across the Bosphorus. Screen: one witness, a book. Which book, and is it true?
2. **Flight 1, Lagâri:** the sultan's daughter is born, fireworks. The sultan watches from his seaside pavilion. Lagâri sits on a gunpowder rocket.
3. **How:** the powder pushes him up. When it runs out he opens his wings, glides, lands in the sea in front of the pavilion. Reward: coins and a salary.
4. **Flight 2, Hezarfen:** from the top of Galata Tower, on the lodos wind, he glides across the Bosphorus to Üsküdar.
5. **Link:** same sultan, same pavilion.
6. **Twist:** Hezarfen gets gold, then exile. Reason: "this man is to be feared."
7. **Answer:** the one writer is Evliya Çelebi. Nobody else.
8. **Close and loop:** "No proof. Yet we've told it for 400 years." Screen asks "Did they really fly?" The spark on the book drawing cuts back to P01.

## 8. Sharing
- **Cover (≤5 words):** TWO FLIGHTS, ONE WITNESS
- **Description:** In the 1630s, under Sultan Murad IV, at the celebration for the birth of the sultan's daughter, Lagâri Hasan Çelebi is said to have flown off Istanbul's Sarayburnu on a gunpowder rocket and landed in the sea in front of the sultan's pavilion. Hezarfen Ahmed Çelebi is said to have glided from the Galata Tower across the Bosphorus to Üsküdar. Both flights are known from one source only: the Ottoman traveler Evliya Çelebi, in his Seyahatname (Book of Travels). Whether Evliya saw them himself is not known, and no other record has been found. (Çelebi is an honorific, "gentleman"; Jesus, Isa, is a prophet in Islam too.) Dates vary by source; the height (~250 m) and distance (~3 km) are modern estimates. The two quotes are Evliya's reported words, simplified; the "Because" linking the sultan's line to the exile is added for the narrative flow. All scenes and gestures, including the helper's hat and the dropped bundle, are dramatization. The voice is AI-generated. Sources: TDV Islamic Encyclopedia (M. Kaçar, "Hasan Çelebi, Lâgarî" and "Hezarfen Ahmed Çelebi"), Küre Encyclopedia.
