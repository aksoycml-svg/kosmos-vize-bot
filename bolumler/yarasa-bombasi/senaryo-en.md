## 0. Turn, spine, ending

**Turn line:** Before: The weapon was going to burn Japan's wooden cities. / After: Its first big fire was at its own base. **Type: reversal** (the irony comes from the event: the weapon worked as designed, only on the wrong roof).

**Protagonist: Dr. Lytle Adams (a dentist).** Why: he carries the want and the obstacle, and viewers follow a person's intent. Making the bat swarm the hero turns the story into a victim story and pushes a war-and-animals topic toward jokes. The swarm stays the instrument and the object of the event; no harm is shown on screen. Weak spot: Adams has no documented role at the Carlsbad fire, so he is not placed there. His **notebook** carries him (P02 set up, P03 drawn, P12 the drawing becomes a real, burned roof). His later fate is not in the research and is not told.

**Five-line spine**
1. **Situation:** A month after Pearl Harbor, a Pennsylvania dentist (Adams) watches bats leave Carlsbad Caverns and notices something.
2. **Want:** Turn bats into a weapon that roosts under the roofs of wooden Japanese houses and starts many fires at once.
3. **Obstacle:** Control. A bat picks its own roost; nobody can pick it for the bat (the dark side of P03's rule).
4. **Change:** On May 15, 1943, armed bats escape by accident at an auxiliary air base, hunt for dark corners, and roost even under a fuel tank; the capsules ignite.
5. **Result:** The project goes on; Admiral King cancels it as too slow to be ready; it is never used, and its best-known fire burns its own base.

**Ending first:** "Never used in war. Its best-known fire burned its own base." What it needs: *a bat roosts under a dark roof* (P03, 8.6 s), *a capsule starts a fire* (P05, ~21 s), *the bats are put to sleep and choose their own roost* (P06–P07), *target Japan / what burned* (P01 screen text). Note: P05–P07 sit just past the first third (~21 s); P03 and P01 carry the rule and the target early.

## 8. Publishing
- **Cover:** ITS OWN BASE BURNED  (4 words)
- **Description:** In World War II, a Pennsylvania dentist named Lytle Adams was inspired by the bats of Carlsbad Caverns, and about a month after Pearl Harbor his idea reached the White House. Louis Fieser, who developed napalm, made small incendiary capsules for bats; chilled, sleeping bats were to be packed in canisters, wake when they opened, and roost under roofs. On May 15, 1943, armed bats were released by accident at an auxiliary air base in New Mexico; they roosted under a fuel tank and the test range burned. By some accounts hangars, barracks and a general's car burned too; these details were not confirmed in a primary source. The project passed to the Navy and then the Marine Corps; by one account it was tested on a village of Japanese-house replicas at Dugway, Utah. About $2 million is said to have been spent. Admiral King cancelled it because it would be ready mid-1945 at the earliest, and it was never used in the war. Sources mostly trace to Jack Couffer's "Bat Bomb" (1992); full texts were not available, so numbers and some details are given softly. Scenes, the notebook and the letter are dramatization; no harm to animals is shown. Narration is AI-generated.
