Source: `arastirma.md` only (search-summary level; no primary document was seen). Channel: EN. Twin of `senaryo.md`: same 12 plans, same timing, same visuals. Only voice and on-screen text change. Visuals are rendered once, without text; screen text is a separate EN layer.

## 0. Turn, spine, ending

**Turn line:** Before: The weapon was going to burn Japan's wooden cities. / After: Its first big fire was at its own base. **Type: reversal** (the irony comes from the event: the weapon worked as designed, only on the wrong roof).

**Protagonist: Dr. Lytle Adams (a dentist).** Why: he carries the want and the obstacle, and viewers follow a person's intent. Making the bat swarm the hero turns the story into a victim story and pushes a war-and-animals topic toward jokes. The swarm stays the instrument and the object of the event; no harm is shown on screen. Weak spot: Adams has no documented role at the Carlsbad fire, so he is not placed there. His **notebook** carries him (P02 set up, P03 drawn, P12 the drawing becomes a real, burned roof). His later fate is not in the research and is not told.

**Five-line spine**
1. **Situation:** A month after Pearl Harbor, a Pennsylvania dentist (Adams) watches bats leave Carlsbad Caverns and notices something.
2. **Want:** Turn bats into a weapon that roosts under the roofs of wooden Japanese houses and starts many fires at once.
3. **Obstacle:** Control. When a chilled, sleeping bat wakes up cannot be set.
4. **Change:** On May 15, 1943, armed bats escape by accident, hunt for dark corners, and roost even under a fuel tank at a new base; the capsules ignite.
5. **Result:** The project goes on; Admiral King cancels it as too slow to be ready; it is never used, and its best-known fire burns its own base.

**Ending first:** "Never used in war. Its best-known fire burned its own base." What it needs: *a bat roosts under a dark roof* (P03, 8.6 s), *a capsule starts a fire* (P05, ~21 s), *the bats are put to sleep and wake on their own clock* (P06–P07), *target Japan / what burned* (P01 screen text). Note: P05–P07 sit just past the first third (~21 s); P03 and P01 carry the rule and the target early.

## 8. Publishing
- **Cover:** ITS OWN BASE BURNED  (4 words)
- **Description:** In World War II, a Pennsylvania dentist named Lytle Adams was inspired by the bats of Carlsbad Caverns, and about a month after Pearl Harbor his idea reached the White House. Louis Fieser, who developed napalm, made small incendiary capsules for bats; chilled, sleeping bats were to be packed in canisters, wake when they opened, and roost under roofs. On May 15, 1943, armed bats were released by accident at a new airfield in New Mexico; the base's new buildings and a fuel tank burned. By some accounts a general's car burned too; this detail was not confirmed in a primary source. The project passed to the Navy and then the Marine Corps, was tested at a site in Utah, and an observer called it effective; about $2 million is said to have been spent. Admiral King cancelled it because it would be ready mid-1945 at the earliest, and it was never used in the war. Sources mostly trace to Jack Couffer's "Bat Bomb" (1992); full texts were not available, so numbers and some details are given softly. Scenes, the notebook and the letter are dramatization; no harm to animals is shown. Narration is AI-generated.
