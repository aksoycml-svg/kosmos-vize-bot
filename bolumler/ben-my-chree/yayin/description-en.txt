The aircraft carrier sunk from dry land

Ben-my-Chree was a fast passenger ferry built in 1908 for the Isle of Man. In the First World War the Royal Navy turned her into a seaplane carrier: a hangar was built behind her rear funnel, and her aircraft were lowered into the water by crane. In January 1917 she anchored in the harbour of Kastellorizo, an island held by the French since December 1915. The shore opposite, Kaş, about 2 km away, was Ottoman. According to British records, about two hours after she entered the harbour hidden guns on the shore opened fire; the first two shells fell into the sea, the third hit the ship. Shells wrecked her steering and set the hangar on fire. About 40 minutes after the shelling began the crew abandoned ship; one officer and four men were wounded, nobody died. A few hours later she rolled over and sank in the harbour's shallow water. She was the only aircraft carrier (seaplane carrier) sunk by enemy fire in the First World War. The wreck was raised in 1920 and scrapped in 1923.
The sources disagree: British records give the date as 11 January 1917, while most Turkish sources say 9 January. British sources count four 155 mm and twelve 77 mm guns; in his memoir (Ben Bir Türk Zabitiyim, "I Am a Turkish Officer"), artillery lieutenant Mustafa Ertuğrul (Aker) describes his own battery of four mountain guns.
