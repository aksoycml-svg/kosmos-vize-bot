## 0. Before anything

**Turn line:** Before: the giant ship looks safe in an island harbor, two kilometers of water and a French garrison around her / After: hidden small guns across the water hit her on the third shot; with no rudder and a fire, she sinks within hours and nobody dies. Type: **contrast**.

**Protagonist:** Lieutenant Mustafa Ertuğrul, the artillery officer on the far shore (his role rests on his own memoirs; the narration says "by his own memoirs").

**Five-line spine**
1. **Situation:** Kastellorizo, a French-held island. HMS Ben-my-Chree, a seaplane carrier, at anchor. The mainland is two kilometers away.
2. **Want:** Ertuğrul, by his memoirs, waits on the far shore to hit the ship (inferred from action).
3. **Obstacle:** four small mountain guns against a huge ship; fire opens about two hours after she arrives.
4. **Change:** the third shot hits; later shells break the rudder and set the hangar on fire; the fire spreads across the deck.
5. **Result:** about forty minutes later the crew leaves; nobody dies; she sinks within hours. Ertuğrul writes it down years later.

**Last line (written first):** "Two kilometers of water hadn't kept her safe."
Set up in the first third: "two kilometers" P01/P03 · "looked safe" P03 · "anchor / not moving" P03/P05 · Ertuğrul and "memoirs" P04 · the line from ship to far shore P01 (closed in P10).

## 1. Accuracy decisions (EN specifics; the rest as in TR file §1)
- No day in the narration; screen says "JANUARY 1917" only (EN may say the year on screen). 9 / 11 / 3 January conflict goes in the caption.
- "Only": "the only aircraft carrier ever lost to enemy fire" is the seaplane-carrier class qualifier; P02 explains it in one line. Not "the first ever sunk".
- "Hidden guns": firm (guns were moved in secretly). "Four small mountain guns": only "by his own memoirs" (Wikipedia gives 4×155 mm + 12×77 mm).
- Ertuğrul's presence is single-source: it sits inside an attributed clause. No sentence says "Ertuğrul sank her".
- Firm: fire about two hours after arrival; third shot hit; rudder and hangar; one working motorboat at about forty minutes; nobody died, five wounded (screen); she sank in shallow water.
- Not used: "36 minutes", 145/110, "fuel-laden hangar", cat and dog, the unsourced gun-wedge legend, "Dard", Samson, the court-martial.
- Respect: no triumphal tone; casualties and "nobody died" are foregrounded.
- Voice: the ship's name (Manx) may be misread by TTS; test one line, phonetic fallback "Ben-my-Kree".

## 8. Publishing
- **Cover (max 5 words):** TWO KILOMETERS WEREN'T ENOUGH
- **Description:** In January 1917 the British seaplane carrier HMS Ben-my-Chree lay at anchor in the French-held harbor of Kastellorizo when Ottoman artillery on the Turkish coast opened fire. British sources record that fire began about two hours after she arrived, that the third shot hit, that the rudder was disabled and the hangar caught fire. The crew left in the one working motorboat; nobody was killed, five were wounded. She is remembered as the only aircraft carrier lost to enemy fire in war. The role of Lt. Mustafa Ertuğrul and the four small mountain guns come from his own memoirs; British sources give a larger number of guns (4×155 mm + 12×77 mm), and the date (11 January in British records, mostly 9 January in Turkish sources), the length of the sinking and the hit counts also vary.
