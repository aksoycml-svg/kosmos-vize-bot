## ENGLISH (~60 s)
1. The first cyberattack in history had no computers. No electricity.
2. Just a tower... and a bribe.
3. France, the 1830s. Twin brothers François and Louis Blanc are betting on government bonds in Bordeaux.
4. The problem: news from the Paris stock exchange takes three days to arrive by mail.
5. The state's optical telegraph can carry it in a few hours. But the telegraph is for the government only.
6. They tried carrier pigeons first. It failed.
7. So they bought the telegraph operators in Tours.
8. Their man in Paris mailed a parcel to Tours: gloves meant the market went up. Socks meant it went down.
9. The operator slipped a tiny "error" into an official message.
10. The error travelled tower to tower to Bordeaux... where a telescope was waiting.
11. For two years, the brothers knew first.
12. Until an operator fell gravely ill and spilled the secret.
13. In 1837 they stood trial. And walked free. There was no law against it.
14. That same year, France made the telegraph a state monopoly by law.
15. And François? Years later, he was running the casino in Monte Carlo.
16. Because in gambling, the winner is always the one who knows first.
