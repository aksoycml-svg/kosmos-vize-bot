## 0. Read first

**Turn line:** Before: the valet who sells secrets thinks he is getting rich / After: most of the money is fake, and his secrets were not believed. Type: **reversal** (ends on a reveal: who printed the money).

**Hero:** Elyesa Bazna (code name Cicero), valet to the British ambassador in Ankara.

**Five-line spine**
1. **Situation:** Ankara, 1943. Bazna serves the British ambassador, whose secret papers sit in a locked box in the bedroom.
2. **Want:** Money, by selling the papers to the Germans.
3. **Obstacle:** The locked box; then Berlin's distrust; finally the fear of being caught.
4. **Change:** He opens the box, photographs, sells. Berlin leaves most of it unused, most of the pay is fake, the leak is noticed, he quits in fear.
5. **Outcome:** He learns later that the Germans who doubted his secrets had printed the fake money.

**Last line (written first):** "He learned later: the Germans who doubted his secrets had printed the fake money."
Setups: fake money P01 · Germany P01 · secrets are real P04 · Berlin doubts P06 · white banknote P01/P05/P07. (Berlin's doubt lands at ~50%, not in the first third; it is a consequence of the sale, so it cannot come earlier.)

## 8. Publishing
- **Cover:** PAID IN FAKE MONEY (4 words)
- **Description:** Elyesa Bazna, code name Cicero, was valet to the British ambassador in Ankara in 1943–44. He is said to have photographed the ambassador's secret papers and sold them to Moyzisch of the German embassy. Most of the roughly £300,000 he was paid is said to have been forged sterling from Germany's Operation Bernhard (how much was fake varies by source). How much Berlin used is also disputed; Britannica says most of it, Normandy included, was disregarded. Details such as the wax impression, the bathroom and the car handovers come from Bazna's and Moyzisch's own accounts, both self-interested. Scenes and gestures are dramatized.
